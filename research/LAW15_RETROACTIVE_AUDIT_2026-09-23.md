## EXP080 — G2 oracle ceiling (preregistered 2026-09-23, LOG-172)

1. **Precise question.** In the output-side candidate pool beyond the bridge
   itself, does a *selection prize* exist — i.e., is there at least one
   candidate in bin (ii) (non-bridge candidates) that rescues baseline-missed
   items at a rate surviving the pre-registered margin?
2. **Decision changed.** CONTINUE: bin (ii) > 0 at the registered margin
   licenses the evaluator/search program (DPRS, Sprint 3 selectors) to
   continue — the room is worth searching. KILL: bin (ii) = 0 kills the
   autonomous-selector route for this family; PIVOT to the K1 readout-tilt
   falsification and CLLC closed-loop bets (per LOG-198 KEEP item 4).
3. **Cheapest.** ~1,320 passes on the Kaggle T4×2 lane, a small slice of the
   weekly 30 GPU-hour budget. The alternative — building search machinery
   (NTDP program, trained selectors) before measuring whether anything exists
   to find — costs orders of magnitude more. No archived dataset records
   per-candidate readout-rescue margins, so a $0 archived-data answer does not
   exist; EXP080 *is* the cheapest sufficient screen.
4. **Mathematical license.** Decision-theoretic necessity lemma [FACT of
   logic]: if no candidate in the pool ever beats baseline, no selector can
   ever find one (contrapositive). The oracle ceiling is an upper bound by
   construction. **Breaking point:** oracle selection uses test labels, so
   EXP080 licenses *existence only*, never *discoverability* — that claim
   belongs to EXP081/DPRS. License grade: logical-necessity lemma, not a
   mechanism theorem. Recorded honestly.

**Audit verdict: PASS.** All four answers on record. Held in queue pending
LOG-197 + CEO §H7 ruling + CEO GPU clearance (per LOG-198 ordering).
