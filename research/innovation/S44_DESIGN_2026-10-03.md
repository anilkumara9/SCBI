## 4. Mathematical license

### 4.1 Banach [THEOREM] + contraction [CONJECTURE]

**[THEOREM — FACT]** Banach's fixed-point theorem: on a complete metric space, a contraction (Lipschitz κ < 1) has a unique fixed point, and iteration converges to it from any start, with ‖h^{(t)} − h∞‖ ≤ κᵗ/(1−κ)·‖h^{(1)} − h^{(0)}‖.

**[CONJECTURE]** R as defined above is a contraction (κ < 1) in Euclidean norm on the residual states visited.

### 4.2 Where contraction could come from — and the honest prior

**[INTERPRETATION]** There is no theorem giving κ < 1 for transformer prefixes. Each Pythia block is x ↦ x + Attn(LN(x)) + MLP(LN(x)) (parallel residual [FACT] from config) — residual structure is generically expansive (Lipschitz ≥ 1, typically > 1), and the residual-stream norm grows across layers in practice. Π = W_EᵀW_U is not an orthogonal projection (‖Π‖₂ may exceed 1). The contraction, if it exists, would have to come from the *composition* — e.g., saturation/attentional averaging damping perturbations along the trajectory — and can only be established empirically.

**Honest prior: κ̂ ≥ 1.** This matches R6's program note ("self-killing by its own math; honest prior is divergence") and general transformer-Lipschitz behavior. The S4 doc's own prediction (κ̂ < 1) is the *candidate's* Law #15 card, not an established fact. The design is falsification-first precisely because the prior is against it: the 40-pass screen exists to kill the license cheaply.

### 4.3 Quantitative predictions and breaking points

**Predictions (the candidate's, pre-registered):** Stage-1 κ̂ < 1; Stage-2 ΔM_attractor ≥ +8pp with paired LCI > 0.05 (Newcombe paired interval on the 60-item bench).

**Breaking points (binding):**
- Stage-1 κ̂ ≥ 1 → **contraction license LOST**: Stage 2 does not run; go to Stage-1b bounded-T pilot.
- Stage-1b ΔM_bounded ≤ 0 → **KILL**.
- Stage-2 ΔM_attractor ≤ 0 → **KILL** (converges to nothing useful).
- Stage-2 0 < ΔM_attractor < +8pp or LCI straddles 0.05 → **HELD**.

### 4.4 One-sided-screen honesty

**[INTERPRETATION]** κ̂ is a *lower bound* on the true Lipschitz constant (max over a finite sample ≤ sup). Therefore κ̂ ≥ 1 refutes contraction, but κ̂ < 1 does **not** prove it — Stage 1 is a necessary-condition screen, not a contraction certificate. If κ̂ < 1, Stage 2 additionally reports the trajectory diagnostic (‖h^{(t+1)} − h^{(t)}‖ strictly decreasing over t = 0..7) as empirical support; a non-decreasing trajectory voids the Banach reading even if ΔM > 0 (re-scoped to PIVOT: bounded refinement without the attractor claim).

## 9. Risks and honest limitations

1. **Honest prior is against contraction** (§4.2). The most likely Stage-1 outcome is κ̂ ≥ 1 → the value is the cheap kill + the bounded-T pilot, not the attractor.
2. **One-sided screen** (§4.4): κ̂ < 1 never proves contraction; the trajectory diagnostic is the backstop.
3. **Π is a modeling choice**, not a derived necessity. Other fixed feedback maps exist (e.g., W_UᵀW_U pseudoinverse-style, identity). The design pre-registers one; alternatives are deviations, not improvisations.
4. **Prior-art overlap** (§8): the bounded-T PIVOT branch swims in 2602.14759's waters — fine for boundary science, not a novelty claim.
5. **Bench specificity:** all bars are on the 60-item decision bench at Pythia-410m. No claim about other benches/backbones is licensed under any branch.
6. **k is fixed at full depth** by pre-registration; shorter loops are a separate design, not a fallback.
