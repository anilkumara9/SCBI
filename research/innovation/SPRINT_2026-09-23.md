## Idea 1 — Compositional factorization: do 1-hop contrast directions add up to 2-hop reasoning?

**The insight.** [CONJECTURE] Multi-hop relational reasoning factorizes in
representation space: the contrast direction for a 2-hop query
("X's capital's country") is approximately the *sum* of the 1-hop contrast
directions for its component relations ("X's capital", "Y's country"). If
v(R1∘R2) ≈ v(R1) + v(R2) and the composed injection rescues 2-hop items that
neither component rescues alone, then compositional generalization happens
through vector arithmetic in the residual stream — not solely through the
model's own forward computation. That would change how people see LLMs: the
residual stream as an algebra of relational operators, with multi-hop
reasoning decomposable into additive parts.

**Why it's not in the literature.** Closest prior: Todd et al., Function Vectors
(ICLR 2024; audit §2.7) — task vectors "partially composable by addition."
Delta: (a) Todd's vectors are extracted from ICL demonstrations via causal
mediation on simple tasks; ours are *contrast directions* from support pairs,
tested on *multi-hop relational reasoning*; (b) Todd shows addition preserves
task behavior — we test whether addition *composes* sub-relations into a
super-relation the model currently fails, with decision-change endpoints;
(c) no verified record tests additive composition of steering/contrast
directions for 2-hop transfer. CAA/ActAdd never compose directions.

**The kill criterion.** On headroom-verified 2-hop items: ΔM(v1+v2) ≤
max(ΔM(v1), ΔM(v2)) with McNemar p ≥ 0.05 for the paired difference, while the
output bridge rescues significantly → no compositional factorization; the
idea is dead.

**Experiment sketch (EXP069 candidate).** Model: pythia-410m, layer 20 (program
continuity). Precondition: construct 1-hop contrast support pairs for each
component relation of the 2-hop benchmark (benchmark-construction requirement,
logged per Law #8). Conditions: C1 unintervened; C2 v(R1); C3 v(R2);
C4 v(R1)+v(R2) (normalized); C5 B_agg (2-hop aggregate, the CAA-equivalent
benchmark); C6 B_perp; C7 B_wrong; C8 output bridge. Endpoint: ΔM on 2-hop
items only, McNemar exact, headroom gate 40–70% carried over. Secondary:
cos(v(R1∘R2)_measured, v(R1)+v(R2)) — geometric check that the sum points
where the measured 2-hop direction points.

**Free-tier cost.** ~8 conditions × 60 items ≈ 500 forward passes on 410m ≈
**< 30 min T4**. Trivial.

## Idea 2 — Oracle-selection ceiling: measure the verifier's headroom before building the generator

**The insight.** [CONJECTURE] For representation-level search, the maximum
achievable gain of *any* target-free evaluator is bounded above by
oracle (label-informed) selection over the same candidate set. Measuring that
ceiling first decides whether the EXP068 loop can possibly work: if even the
oracle cannot beat static injection, no target-free evaluator can, and the
loop hypothesis is dead on arrival — saving the entire loop implementation.
If the oracle *can* beat it, the gap between oracle and static quantifies
exactly how much verifier quality is worth, turning "build a better
evaluator" from a hope into a measured prize. Applied generally, this inverts
the field's build order for test-time compute: verifier-first, generator-second.

**Why it's not in the literature.** Closest prior: Brown et al., Large Language
Monkeys (2024; audit §2.17) — coverage scales with samples but selection
plateaus without verifiers. Delta: (a) Brown et al. study *output* sampling
coverage; we apply the ceiling argument to *basis-valued candidates* with an
explicit oracle-selection measurement; (b) they never test whether
oracle-selection over representation candidates has headroom — the exact
quantity that gates SCBI's loop; (c) framed as a *kill-the-loop* pre-test,
it is a methodological contribution to test-time-compute experimental design,
not just an observation.

**The kill criterion (for the loop, not the idea).** Oracle Best-of-K
(label-informed argmax-rescue per instance over the loop spec's G1 candidates,
K = 8) yields ΔM_oracle ≤ ΔM_static with McNemar p ≥ 0.05 → no evaluator,
however good, can justify the loop; H_loop is dead without building it.

**Experiment sketch (EXP070 candidate; runs before/without EXP068).**
Model: pythia-160m (cheap) then 410m. Candidates: G1 bootstrap-resampled
contrast directions (loop spec §2.1), K = 8 per instance, N = 60 headroom
items. Conditions: C1 unintervened; C2 static B_agg; C3 oracle-selected
per-instance direction (labels used — permitted: this is a *ceiling
measurement*, not a proposed method; Law #7's test-time boundary applies to
the loop, not to the ceiling diagnostic); C4 random-selected candidate
(controls for "any selection works"); C5 B_wrong; C6 output bridge. Endpoint:
ΔM, McNemar; secondary: distribution of per-instance oracle gains (is the
ceiling concentrated in a rescuable subset?).

**Free-tier cost.** K × N = 480 candidate evaluations + conditions ≈ 1,000
forward passes ≈ **< 1 h T4** (160m). Cheap enough to run first, always.

## Idea 3 — Target-free steerability diagnostic: cross-view agreement predicts rescue

**The insight.** [CONJECTURE] Whether a *fixed* steering direction induces the
same decision across vocabulary-instantiated views of one query predicts,
without any labels, whether that direction can rescue that instance. If
corr(C_cons(B_agg; x), 1[rescue]) > 0 on held-out support, the field gains a
label-free triage tool: run the cheap agreement diagnostic, steer only the
instances it flags, and stop wasting intervention budget where steering
cannot work. It also standalone-validates the E-component of the loop
spec — the ρ-gate as its own experiment, decoupled from G/S/T.

**Why it's not in the literature.** Closest prior: Wang et al.,
Self-Consistency (ICLR 2023; audit §2.16) — agreement over sampled reasoning
paths as an accuracy proxy. Delta: (a) our agreement is over
*intervention-induced decisions across input views*, not over sampled outputs;
(b) the predicted target is *causal efficacy of an intervention* (will this
steer work?), not answer correctness — a different prediction problem with no
verified prior; (c) Brown et al.'s verifier discussion is about selecting
among outputs, not triaging steerability.

**The kill criterion.** Point-biserial corr(C_cons, rescue) on D_gate-style
held-out pairs: |r| < 0.2 or p ≥ 0.05 → agreement carries no signal about
steerability; the diagnostic (and the loop's E in its current form) is dead.

**Experiment sketch (EXP071 candidate).** Model: pythia-410m, layer 20. Views:
m = 5 entity-frame substitutions per instance (loop spec §2.2 construction).
For each of N = 60 headroom items: compute C_cons(B_agg; x) over the 5 views
(target-free), then measure actual rescue (labels used only for the
correlation measurement, never by the diagnostic). Conditions for the rescue
measurement: static B_agg vs unintervened. Controls: same diagnostic computed
for B_wrong (must show no correlation — discriminant validity) and B_perp.
Endpoint: point-biserial r with exact permutation p; pre-register r ≥ 0.3 as
the "signal exists" bar [ARBITRARY — sensitivity analysis required].

**Free-tier cost.** m × N × 2 conditions ≈ 600 forward passes ≈ **< 30 min T4**.

## Idea 4 — The anti-geometry hypothesis: the idiosyncratic component transfers, the shared one doesn't

**The insight.** [CONJECTURE] Our boundary result (~0.7 shared cosine, zero
transfer) may have the sign wrong: the *shared* component across vocabulary
contrast directions could be exactly the anisotropic bias direction every
model carries (Ethayarajh 2019; Jorgensen et al. 2023) — geometrically large,
causally empty — while the *idiosyncratic* residual per vocabulary carries
the actual relational signal. Decompose v̂_k = v̄ + r_k; inject r_k (the
vocabulary-specific residual) instead of v̄. If idiosyncratic components
transfer where the shared aggregate doesn't, the field's "shared geometry =
transferable knowledge" assumption is inverted for steering: what generalizes
is the residue, not the common core.

**Why it's not in the literature.** Closest prior: Jorgensen et al. (2023;
audit §2.8) — mean-centring removes the shared offset to *improve* steering.
Delta: (a) Jorgensen discards the shared component as bias; we test the
stronger claim that the *residual* is the causal carrier, on *cross-vocabulary
transfer* with decision endpoints — Jorgensen tested behavior steering, not
transfer; (b) Bansal et al. (stitching; audit §2.14) never decompose shared
vs. idiosyncratic geometry; (c) no verified record tests
residual-component injection for causal transfer.

**The kill criterion.** Idiosyncratic injection ΔM = 0 (b = c = 0, McNemar
p ≥ 0.05) while the output bridge rescues → no inversion; the shared-null
stands and the residue is noise.

**Experiment sketch (EXP072 candidate).** Model: pythia-410m, layer 20.
Decomposition per vocabulary k: r_k = v̂_k − v̄ (v̄ = mean direction).
Conditions: C1 unintervened; C2 B_agg (shared, CAA-equivalent); C3 pooled
idiosyncratic r = normalize(Σ r_k); C4 per-vocabulary r_k on vocabulary-k
items (matched test); C5 mean-centred B_agg (Idea 5's condition — run jointly);
C6 B_perp; C7 B_wrong; C8 output bridge. Endpoint: ΔM, McNemar, headroom
40–70%. Secondary: ‖r_k‖/‖v̂_k‖ ratios (is there enough residual energy for
the hypothesis to be physically plausible? if residuals are tiny, the idea
fails on energetics before causality).

**Free-tier cost.** ~8 conditions × 60 items ≈ 500 forward passes ≈
**< 30 min T4**. Vector arithmetic is free.

## Idea 5 — Centred-basis rescue: apply the Jorgensen correction the program omitted

**The insight.** [CONJECTURE] The program's own literature audit (§2.8) flags
that SCBI never mean-centred its contrast directions, while Jorgensen et al.
(2023) establish that raw mean-difference vectors carry a non-informative
global offset — and Ethayarajh (2019) that anisotropy inflates raw cosines.
Our "zero causal transfer" boundary result may therefore be a *hygiene
artifact*, not a mechanism verdict: the ~0.7 cosine may be mostly shared
anisotropic bias, and the task-relevant component may be buried underneath
it. Re-running static injection with centred directions either overturns our
own headline null (high value: it corrects the program's central claim) or
confirms the null under best-practice hygiene (high value: the boundary
result becomes substantially harder to dismiss). Either way the experiment
pays.

**Why it's not in the literature.** Closest prior: Jorgensen et al. (2023;
audit §2.8) — mean-centring for activation steering. Delta: (a) Jorgensen
tested behaviour steering (sycophancy-style); we test *cross-vocabulary
relational transfer* with decision-change endpoints — the exact setting of
our boundary result; (b) framed as an *adversarial re-test of our own null*,
it is the experiment the audit's red-team demands (Challenge 5's rebuttal
notes the missing anisotropy baselines — this supplies them).

**The kill criterion (for the hygiene hypothesis).** Centred B_agg still
yields ΔM = 0 (b = c = 0, McNemar p ≥ 0.05) on headroom-verified items →
the null is not a hygiene artifact; the boundary result survives best
practice and is strengthened.

**Experiment sketch (EXP073 candidate; mergeable with EXP072).**
Model: pythia-410m, layer 20. Centring: subtract the support-corpus mean
activation (Jorgensen-style) from each v̂_k before aggregation; also test
per-vocabulary centring. Conditions: C1 unintervened; C2 raw B_agg; C3
global-centred B_agg; C4 per-vocab-centred B_agg; C5 B_perp (centred);
C6 B_wrong; C7 output bridge. Endpoint: ΔM, McNemar. Secondary (the
anisotropy control the audit demands): cosine of raw vs. centred directions
against random-direction baselines — quantifying how much of the 0.7 was
bias.

**Free-tier cost.** ~7 conditions × 60 items ≈ 450 forward passes ≈
**< 30 min T4**.

## Idea 6 — Layer-of-causality map: the extraction layer is not the causal layer

**The insight.** [CONJECTURE] The program injected at the layer where the
contrast direction was *measured* (L10/160m, L20/410m), conflating
measurability with causal efficacy. Relational binding in a 2-hop query may
be computed — and hence steerable — at a different depth than where its
contrast direction is most visible. A full layer-swept causal map of a fixed
direction (ΔM per layer, with the output bridge as a per-layer positive
control) would either localize relational causality to a specific depth band
(a mechanistic result about *where* in the network relations live) or show
zero transfer at every layer (strengthening the boundary result from "not at
L20" to "not anywhere").

**Why it's not in the literature.** Closest prior: Rimsky et al., CAA
(2023/ACL 2024; audit §2.3) — layer sweeps for steering effectiveness. Delta:
(a) CAA sweeps layers to *optimize* behaviour steering; we sweep to *map the
causal geography* of a fixed relational direction, with per-layer positive
controls distinguishing "layer is causally dead" from "direction is causally
dead"; (b) Todd et al. (function vectors; audit §2.7) localize task vectors
via causal mediation but never test cross-vocabulary transfer per layer;
(c) no verified record pairs a layer-swept causal map with the
measurability-vs-efficacy distinction on relational reasoning.

**The kill criterion.** ΔM = 0 at *every* sampled layer (b = c = 0 throughout)
while the output bridge rescues at ≥ 1 layer → location is not the
explanation; the null generalizes across depth.

**Experiment sketch (EXP074 candidate).** Model: pythia-410m (32 layers);
sample every 4th layer (8 points) for the pilot, densify around any signal.
Per layer: inject B_agg; per-layer output bridge (positive control proving
the layer is intervenable); shared controls B_perp, B_wrong, unintervened
run once. Endpoint: ΔM per layer, McNemar; headroom gate per layer
(headroom must hold at each sampled layer or the layer is excluded, logged).
Extension (if budget allows): × token position (entity slots vs. answer
position) — the "computational staging" variant.

**Free-tier cost.** Pilot: 8 layers × (60 + 60 bridge + shared controls ≈ 200)
≈ 1,600 forward passes ≈ **~1 h T4**. Full 32-layer map ≈ 4 h — still within
a week of free quota.

## Idea 7 — Subspace-restricted bridge: the subspace is right, the direction is wrong

**The insight.** [CONJECTURE] The five per-vocabulary contrast directions span
a k=5 relational subspace S that *is* causally relevant — but B_agg, their
average, points the wrong way *inside* it. Test: restrict the known-working
output bridge to S — inject P_S(bridge_vec) instead of the full bridge. If
the subspace-restricted bridge rescues as well as the full bridge, then S
contains a causal direction and the program's problem was never the geometry
but the *choice of direction within it* — which is exactly the search problem
the EXP068 loop is built to solve. A positive result here would be the
strongest possible motivation for the loop: proof that the treasure is in
the room, we just haven't searched it.

**Why it's not in the literature.** Closest prior: ReFT/LoReFT (Wu et al.
2024; audit §2.20) — low-rank subspace interventions on frozen models. Delta:
(a) ReFT *learns* subspace edits by training; ours is a *heuristic*
contrast-derived subspace tested with zero training; (b) no verified record
tests whether a steering-relevant subspace contains a causal direction by
projecting a known-working intervention into it; (c) DAS (Geiger et al. 2024;
audit §2.15) learns rotations against intervention objectives — adjacent in
spirit, opposite in method (learned vs. constructed).

**The kill criterion.** P_S(bridge) yields ΔM = 0 (McNemar p ≥ 0.05) while the
full bridge rescues → the contrast subspace itself is causally irrelevant,
not just mispointed; the loop would be searching an empty room.

**Experiment sketch (EXP075 candidate).** Model: pythia-410m, layer 20.
S = span{v̂_1..v̂_5} (orthonormalized). Conditions: C1 unintervened;
C2 full output bridge; C3 P_S(bridge) (subspace-restricted); C4 P_S⊥(bridge)
(complement-restricted — discriminant: if *this* works and C3 doesn't, the
causal direction was never in S); C5 B_agg; C6 B_perp; C7 B_wrong. Endpoint:
ΔM, McNemar; secondary: ‖P_S(bridge)‖/‖bridge‖ (energy retained — if the
projection keeps <10% of the norm, a null is uninformative about S and the
test is re-designed, not failed). Headroom 40–70%.

**Free-tier cost.** ~7 conditions × 60 items ≈ 450 forward passes ≈
**< 30 min T4**.

## ★ Wildest credible — Cross-model causal transfer: do different-sized LLMs share one relational geometry?

**Kill criterion first.** Fit a sound, same-space, full-rank linear map W from
pythia-160m's residual stream (layer 10, d=768) to pythia-410m's (layer 20,
d=1024) on anchor activations; map 160m's relational contrast direction into
410m's space; inject at 410m-L20. If ΔM_mapped = 0 (McNemar p ≥ 0.05) while
the native 410m output bridge rescues → no cross-model causal transfer; the
idea is dead and the boundary extends across model scale.

**The insight.** [CONJECTURE] If a relational contrast direction computed in
a 160M model, transported by a well-conditioned linear map into a 410M
model's residual stream, *causes decision changes there*, then LLMs of
different scales share a common causal relational geometry — convergent
internal structure at the mechanistic level, not just similar benchmark
scores. That would change how people see LLMs: scale would look less like
"different minds" and more like "different resolutions of the same mind."
A clean null is equally valuable: it would show relational geometry is
model-idiosyncratic even across the same family and training data —
a boundary on universality claims.

**Why it's not in the literature.** Closest prior: Bansal, Nakkiran & Barak,
Model Stitching (NeurIPS 2021; audit §2.14) — align-then-test-transfer
between networks. Delta, all load-bearing: (a) stitching transports *full
activations* to test *task performance*; we transport a *compact causal
direction* to test *decision changes* — a strictly stronger and different
claim (a direction is a far narrower object than an activation vector);
(b) cross-*scale* within one model family on *relational reasoning* is
untested — stitching is cross-architecture; (c) we use the sound same-space
full-rank operator class (EXP067 machinery), not the rank-deficient
cross-space fit the audit already killed; (d) no verified record in the
24-record audit tests cross-model steering-vector transfer with causal
endpoints. The audit's §8 open follow-ups do not list it either.

**Defense against the hostile reviewer.** "Bansal already showed alignment ≠
causal interchangeability." — Response: Bansal showed it for *activation
stitching*; we test it for *steering directions*, which the literature
treats as the portable unit of behaviour (CAA, ActAdd, RepE all assume
directions are the meaningful object). A direction that survives cross-model
transport would be the first evidence that the steering literature's core
abstraction is model-universal. "Cross-lingual Procrustes already maps
spaces." — Response: across *languages within one space*, never across
*models*, and never with a causal decision endpoint. "The map will just fit
noise." — Response: the map is fit on anchor *activations* (same prompts,
both models), validated by held-out reconstruction error *before* any
direction is transported; a map that can't reconstruct activations is
rejected and the experiment halts — a reportable outcome, not a silent
confound. Every step fails closed.

**Experiment sketch (EXP076 candidate).** Models: pythia-160m (source) →
pythia-410m (target). Stage 1 (map validation): collect residual activations
at L10/L20 for identical support prompts in both models; fit W (least
squares, full-rank, conditioning guard: reject if κ(W) > threshold
[ARBITRARY — pre-register]); validate on held-out prompts (reconstruction
cosine bar pre-registered). Stage 2 (transfer test): transport 160m's v̂
(relational contrast direction); conditions at 410m-L20: C1 unintervened;
C2 native 410m B_agg; C3 W-transported 160m direction; C4 410m output
bridge; C5 B_perp; C6 B_wrong. Endpoint: ΔM, McNemar, headroom 40–70%.
Secondary: cos(W·v̂_160m, v̂_410m) — geometric agreement independent of
causality.

**Free-tier cost.** Anchor collection ~150 prompts × 2 models + eval ≈
2,000 forward passes, mixed 160m/410m ≈ **2–3 h T4**. Feasible; schedule
across two Kaggle sessions if needed.

### New Idea 1 — Online search vs SVF's KNN retrieval: the adaptive head-to-head [CONJECTURE]

**The question.** Can an *online*, target-free, per-instance direction search
(no pre-trained boundary) match SVF's *training-free KNN retrieval* baseline
at equal displacement budgets on our two-hop relational benchmark? (The
offline-learned field is cited, not run — see below.)

**Why it matters.** Steering Vector Fields (Li et al., Feb 2026,
arXiv:2602.01654, `verified live`) shows that even a *training-free* KNN
baseline — steer toward the centroid of the K=64 nearest target-bank
neighbors of the live hidden state — already beats static CAA at the same
displacement budget on Model-Written-Evals (Llama-2-7b-Chat, layer 15) for
Corrigible/Coordinate (Narcissism remains unsteerable even under KNN). SVF's
own learned field (an MLP concept-scorer trained on preference triplets,
steering along the boundary normal v(h) = ∇f(h)) is the 2026 *adaptive-search
prior* this proposal is measured against; the Procrustes-*alignment* mantle
passes to Gemma Scope 2's cross-layer transcoders (New Idea 4) — the two
mantle claimants are split, not doubled. **Correction (adversarial review,

### New Idea 2 — The loop as a closed-loop controller: A-LQR [CONJECTURE]

**The question.** Does closed-loop feedback control (Jacobian-based LQR) of
residual-stream activations beat generate-evaluate-select search for
per-instance steering — and can our loop be reframed as a controller with
tracking-error bounds?

**Why it matters.** Activation-LQR (Skifstad, Yang & Chou, Apr 2026,
arXiv:2604.19018, `verified live` in abstract) shows layer-wise transformer
dynamics are well-approximated by locally-linear models across architectures
and scales; it adapts the classical linear quadratic regulator to compute
*feedback controllers* from layer-wise Jacobians, steering activations toward
semantic setpoints in closed loop — no offline training, minimal overhead —
with derived bounds on setpoint tracking error (formal steering guarantees)
and claimed SOTA modulation of toxicity, truthfulness, refusal, and
arbitrary concepts across models/scales/tasks. This is the first
control-theoretic steering operator (feedback control vs. open-loop additive
shifts) — the sweep's direction-changing read is that it gives adaptive
steering both a theoretical language (setpoints, feedback, error bounds) and
a concrete algorithm for per-instance loops. Our G/E/S/T is an open-loop
search with no error bounds; A-LQR may be the formulation our loop should
have been.

**The cheapest falsifying experiment.** Implement A-LQR on the 2-hop
benchmark: **primary setpoint (pre-registered) = the output-bridge
direction** — the direction we already know is causal, keeping the label
budget symmetric with the loop's oracle fallback; secondary arm = the mean
activation of correct two-hop answers at the target layer (label-informed
only for the *setpoint definition*, never adapted per test instance at eval
time). Per item:
compute the layer-wise Jacobian at the intervention layer (autograd through
one layer), derive the feedback gain, inject the closed-loop correction.
Conditions: C1 unintervened; C2 static B_agg; C3 A-LQR closed-loop; C4
loop-selected (or oracle) direction; C5 B_wrong; C6 output bridge. Pilot on
pythia-160m (cheapest Jacobians) before touching 410m.

**The kill criterion.** Formulation kill: A-LQR rescues significantly
(McNemar p < 0.05 vs C1 on N ≥ 50 headroom items) while our loop-selected
direction does not → generate-evaluate-select is the wrong architecture for
the loop; the G/E/S machinery is dominated by the controller and the loop
spec is revised or withdrawn in its current form. **The formulation kill is
licensed ONLY on the loop-selected arm** (adversarial review, 2026-09-23):
if the loop's oracle fallback is the comparison arm, the same outcome
licenses only the weaker claim "feedback control beats oracle selection" —
a different and weaker conclusion, recorded as such. Reframing kill (the
mirror): A-LQR's tracking-error bounds fail to hold on the relational task
(measured tracking error ≫ the derived bound) AND ΔM_A-LQR = 0 → closed-loop
control is not the answer for relational reasoning; the controller
reframing is withdrawn. **Faithful-but-inert cell** (adversarial review,
2026-09-23): ΔM_A-LQR = 0 with the tracking-error bounds holding → the
controller implements faithfully but is relationally inert; the reframing is
neither killed nor adopted — report as a negative. Success bar for adoption:
ΔM_A-LQR ≥ ΔM_static with p < 0.05 *and* measured tracking error within the
bound's order of magnitude — then error-bound-style evaluation enters the
program's protocol.

**Free-tier cost.** Jacobian per item per layer via autograd ≈ ~2–3× a forward
pass on 160m [CONJECTURE — log actuals on the pilot]; pilot ≈ 60 items ×
(6 conditions + Jacobians) ≈ **~1–2 h T4**. Gate the 410m run on the 160m
pilot's signal.

**Endpoints.** ΔM decision changes, exact McNemar; headroom 40–70%.
Secondary: measured vs. bound tracking error per item (the bound's first
independent check on a relational task).

**N1 framing.** LQR applied to activations is classical control meeting
steering — a known combination, not a new principle. If feedback beats
search, the *program's* contribution becomes the benchmark and the
formulation comparison, not the operator (that credit stays with A-LQR).

### New Idea 3 — Anti-steerability and coherence as mandatory pre-registration gates [CONJECTURE]

**The question.** Do per-sample anti-steerable fractions and coherence
diagnostics change the program's verdicts — is static CAA's failure partly
mismeasurement, and should these become mandatory gates before any
adaptive-vs-static comparison?

**Why it matters.** Braun et al. ("Understanding (Un)Reliability of Steering
Vectors," ICLR 2025 Workshop, arXiv:2505.22637, `verified live` in abstract):
CAA difference-of-means on Llama-2-7B-Chat across 36 binary-choice datasets —
all seven prompt types net-positive but high variance, frequently
opposite-to-intended effects; no prompt type dominates; vectors from
different prompt types are directionally distinct; higher cosine similarity
among training activation differences predicts effective steering;
better-separated (+/−) datasets are more steerable. Conclusion: vector
steering is unreliable when the target behavior is not represented by a
coherent direction — with coherence diagnostics (directional agreement,
separability) as the proposed pre-flight check. Tan et al.
(arXiv:2407.12404, `index`, cited as 2025): anti-steerable fraction (vector
pushes logit-difference opposite to intent) ranges 3–50%, averaging **~1/3**
across 36 datasets — aggregate metrics mask per-sample harm. The sweep's
read: this forces a methodological overhaul — per-sample harm metrics before
any adaptive-vs-static comparison, or the program risks "winning on average
while harming a third of instances."

**The cheapest falsifying experiment.** Zero GPU: re-analyze the archived
EXP065/066 artifacts. Compute per-item anti-steerability under static B_agg
(items flipped correct→wrong vs wrong→correct — the signed per-sample
ledger), the anti-steerable fraction per condition, and Braun-style
coherence diagnostics on our contrast pairs (directional agreement among
training activation differences; separability of the +/− sets). Law #13
compliant: no new test labels are needed beyond recorded outcomes.

**The kill criterion (for the methodological claim).** If the anti-steerable
fraction is ≤ 5% on our 2-hop benchmark AND coherence diagnostics are high
(directional agreement in the top quartile of the Braun 36-dataset reference
— [ARBITRARY] bar, sensitivity analysis required), the unreliability
literature does not apply to our setting: our ΔM = 0 (b = c = 0) stands as a
true per-sample zero, and no gating metric is needed — the proposal's
premise is withdrawn. **Degenerate branch** (adversarial review, 2026-09-23):
b = c = 0 is already established for EXP065/066 static injection, so the
per-item signed ledger is nearly guaranteed to return a 0% anti-steerable
fraction on this artifact — the "premise withdrawn" branch fires trivially.
The open question is coherence, not the fraction; confirming the trivial
zero is still a logged measurement, and the coherence diagnostics plus the
gate-adoption machinery are the real payload. **Middle band** (adversarial
review, 2026-09-23): fraction in (5%, 15%] → report the ledger, no protocol
change; the fraction is carried as a reported sensitivity band for the
standing verdict; no gating metric is adopted and the premise is not
withdrawn — a sensitivity-analysis hold. If the fraction is substantial
(> 15% — [ARBITRARY] gate; 1/3 is the literature average), the gate is
*adopted into the protocol*: every future EXP068/070-style comparison
pre-registers a per-sample harm ledger, and "beats on average" without a
harm report is ruled inadmissible evidence. **Feasibility check before
scheduling** (adversarial review, 2026-09-23): verify per-instance signed
outcome ledgers exist in the EXP065/066 archive — if only aggregate McNemar
tables were archived, the fraction is uncomputable and the proposal reduces
to coherence diagnostics on contrast pairs. **Sequencing consequence:** if
the >15% branch fires, the *signed* EXP068/070 evaluators need a per-sample
harm-ledger amendment → Law #14 re-review before execution. P3 runs before
any EXP068/070 execution.

**Free-tier cost.** **$0 GPU** — pure re-analysis of archived
activations/instance logs. The cheapest falsification in this sprint.

**Endpoints.** Per-sample signed outcome ledgers (not margins — the ledger is
decision changes, which already satisfies O5). Gate adoption is a protocol
decision, not a steering endpoint.

**N1 framing.** Diagnostic adoption is methodology, not a mechanism claim;
no novelty tier moves. Its value is making every future claim harder to
fool — including our own.

### New Idea 5 — The adaptivity tournament: which form of adaptivity wins under Δθ=0? [CONJECTURE]

**The question.** Under frozen parameters, which form of adaptivity wins for
relational rescue — gated vectors (CAST), vector fields (SVF), closed-loop
control (A-LQR), or entropy-gated compute (EAGER-port)?

**Why it matters.** The sweep's finding #5: conditionality is now table
stakes. CAST (Lee & Padhi, ICLR 2025, `index` — paper Sep 2024): a *condition
vector* (prompt-induced activation pattern) gates whether the behavior
vector fires via live-activation similarity — selective refusal at
activation-steering cost, shipped in IBM's steering toolkit. WAS ("Guiding
Giants," Hegazy et al., May 2025, revised Mar 2026, `verified live` in
abstract): a lightweight controller observes intermediate activations and
predicts a global scale plus layer-specific weights modulating a
pre-computed direction — Llama-3.1-8B refusal ~89% with MMLU/HellaSwag/
AlpacaEval preserved. EAGER (Scalena et al., Cohere, Oct 2025, `index`):
training-free entropy-gated per-instance compute — branches only at
high-entropy tokens, up to −65% tokens and +37% Pass@k on AIME 2025; its
gating logic ports directly to steering (steer harder where uncertainty is
high). The field has converged: always-on static vectors are the wrong
default. The program's read: static CAA is demoted from "method under test"
to **ablation baseline**; the real comparison is *which adaptivity* wins.
(WAS's *trained* controller is excluded from the tournament — it violates
our frozen-everything identity; only its training-free gating logic is
admissible. First Finish Search's output-level parallel sampling is already
covered by EXP068's output-level search comparison — no duplicate arm.)

**The cheapest falsifying experiment.** A four-way tournament on the
headroom 2-hop set, each method in its cheapest training-free form: CAST
(condition = prompt-induced activation pattern; fire B_agg only when
sim(condition, live activation) > τ, τ pre-registered); SVF-KNN field
(K=64, from New Idea 1); our loop's selection (or EXP070-oracle proxy,
sequenced after EXP068/070); EAGER-port (compute the per-instance steering
budget from answer-token entropy: steer at full α only on high-entropy
instances, α/2 elsewhere — a per-instance *budget*, not a per-instance
direction). Conditions: C1 unintervened; C2 static CAA (ablation);
C3 CAST-gated; C4 SVF-KNN; C5 loop-selected; C6 EAGER-budgeted;
C7 B_wrong; C8 output bridge. Displacement budgets matched where
applicable; the EAGER arm's budget claim is audited by logging actual
per-instance α.

**The kill criterion.** If *no* adaptive method beats the static-CAA ablation
on decision rescue (all ΔM_adaptive ≤ ΔM_static, McNemar p ≥ 0.05) while the
output bridge rescues → adaptivity as such buys nothing on relational
reasoning; the tournament's premise is withdrawn and the boundary claim
hardens from "static doesn't transfer" to "no tested adaptive form
transfers either." If exactly one form wins (ΔM_winner > ΔM_static, p < 0.05,
and > all other adaptive arms), that form becomes the program's new baseline
and the losers are culled from the sprint (Friday cull). **Tie /
multiple-winner rule** (pre-registered, adversarial review, 2026-09-23): if
two or more adaptive arms beat the static ablation with no significant
pairwise difference between them, the joint winners survive to a pairwise
runoff on a fresh headroom split; if the runoff is still tied, the lower-cost
method takes the baseline on free-tier economy.

**Free-tier cost.** CAST gating is one similarity computation per instance;
SVF-KNN is retrieval; EAGER-budgeting is one entropy read per instance —
all near-free atop forward passes. ≈ 8 conditions × 60 items ≈ 500 passes
≈ **< 45 min T4**.

**Endpoints.** ΔM decision changes, exact McNemar; headroom 40–70%.
Secondary: per-instance α ledger for the EAGER arm (was the budget actually
reallocated?).

**N1 framing.** The tournament is methodology; the winner's mechanism
inherits the winner's tier, not a new one. The program's contribution is the
first head-to-head of adaptivity *forms* on one benchmark — an N1
experimental design, honestly labeled.
