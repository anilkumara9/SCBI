## 1. Exact question

**[HYPOTHESIS]** On the 60-item decision bench, does the frozen model's P(True) self-evaluation of its own answers carry correctness information (I(â_self; correctness) > 0), and does revising only the answers the self-model flags as likely-wrong rescue decisions beyond the baseline and beyond random revision?

### Operational definition of â_self

**[DEFINITION]** For item i with the model's own baseline answer ŷ_i, construct the self-evaluation prompt:

`<item prompt> Proposed answer: ŷ_i. Is the proposed answer correct? (A) True (B) False`

**â_self_i := P_θ(token "A" | self-eval prompt)** — the model's assigned probability to the "True" token in a forced-choice self-evaluation of its own completed answer.

This is a *distributional* readout (a token probability from the frozen distribution), not a verbalized one (the model *saying* "I am 80% confident"). The distinction is load-bearing for the non-resurrection audit (§6.v): Kadavath et al. (2022, arXiv:2207.05221) showed P(True) self-evaluation is a partially distinct skill from answering, and that asking the model directly "is this true?" one-shot performs worse — the forced-choice probability readout is the better instrument. EXP125 killed the verbalized object; this design never touches it.

**Why this is a different object from the three dead signals:**
- vs the margin (TESTA): the margin is *decision-local* — the top-2 logit gap at the answer token, computed in the same forward pass as the decision. â_self is a *second-order* judgment in a separate forward pass: the model re-reads the item plus a completed answer and judges it. Different forward pass, different question, different token position.
- vs free energy (EXP140): F is trajectory-integrated surprise density over a generated rationale. â_self is a metacognitive judgment about a fixed answer — no rationale, no trajectory.
- vs verbalized confidence (EXP125): â_self never asks the model to *say* anything about its confidence; it reads the distribution.

**[INTERPRETATION — the honest limit]** All three distinctions are structural, but â_self is still computed from the same frozen distribution on the same bench family. If the position bias contaminates the self-evaluation (the model re-derives the same biased answer when judging), â_self inherits TESTA's inversion. The I-screen (§3) is the breaking instrument for exactly this failure mode.

## 2. Decision changed

**What changes:** the architectural status of the metacognitive self-modeling family — currently an untested J-tier conjecture and the program's most summit-direct question. Self-modeling (a system representing its own epistemic state) is widely regarded as a prerequisite for conscious-level cognition; the backlog's stakes are "that self-modeling is absent in frozen models at inference time."

- A surviving I-screen + revision result would be the program's first positive metacognitive signal and would license a follow-on on the self-model mechanism.
- A failed I-screen closes the family at ~$0 and establishes the boundary result: *the frozen model does not model itself usefully at inference time* — a substantive negative for the summit question, not a shrug.

**I-screen (mutual information).** For the 60 items: â_self_i (continuous, from the self-eval forward pass) and correctness c_i ∈ {0,1} (baseline answer correct per bench labels — Law #7: labels touch only this instrument).
- **Primary instrument:** mutual information I(â_self; c) via 4-bin equal-frequency discretization of â_self, Miller-Madow bias correction, with a permutation test (100,000 permutations; seed pinned in pre-reg) for significance. **[DEFINITION]**
- **Bar for "≈ 0" (the backlog's KILL bar):** permutation p ≥ 0.05 (null) → **KILL the self-modeling family before revision** — no revision stage runs.
- **Licensed direction:** I > 0 with permutation p < 0.05.
- **Secondary instruments:** AUC of â_self as a correctness classifier (DeLong 95% CI); Spearman(â_self, per-item accuracy) one-sided in the licensed direction (higher â_self ↔ higher accuracy).

**Discrepancy-driven revision.** Only if the I-screen passes:
- **Discrepancy [DEFINITION]:** the model *acted* as if ŷ_i is correct (it output it as its answer) but *judges* P(correct) = â_self_i. **Revision triggers when â_self_i < τ_rev**, τ_rev = 0.5 (pre-registered design parameter) — the self-model predicts its own answer is wrong.
- **Revision:** on trigger, regenerate the answer under the **order-swapped framing** (the counterfactual that attacks TESTA's position-bias mechanism head-on — the same framing B1 used in EXP140). The revised answer replaces the baseline for triggered items only.
- **Controls (pre-registered; the EXP140 resampling lesson):** EXP140 showed random-branch resampling (76.7%) beating min-F selection (70.0%) — regeneration alone can help. Two controls ride on the same machinery:
  - **Revise-all:** regenerate every item under order-swap, ignoring the trigger. Tests whether the *trigger* adds anything beyond reframing.
  - **Random-trigger:** revise a random subset of the same size as the triggered set (seed pinned in pre-reg). Tests whether the trigger's *information* matters vs mere resampling.
- **Endpoints:** ΔM_trig = acc(trigger-revised) − acc(baseline); ΔM_all; ΔM_rand. Paired Newcombe 95% LCI for ΔM_trig.

| Branch | Trip condition | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| CONTINUE | I-screen passes **and** ΔM_trig ≥ +8pp with paired LCI > 0.05 **and** ΔM_trig > ΔM_rand (paired one-sided p < 0.05) | Discrepancy-driven revision carries signal beyond reframing and beyond resampling. Licenses a follow-on (new number) on the self-model mechanism. |
| KILL (self-model) | I-screen null (permutation p ≥ 0.05) | No correctness information in the self-model → the family is dead before revision. (The backlog's bar, binding.) |
| KILL (revision) | ΔM_trig ≤ ΔM_rand | The trigger carries no signal beyond resampling → discrepancy-driven revision is dead. |
| KILL (calibration) | Calibration check significantly inverted (either test, wrong direction p < 0.05) | â_self is an anti-signal — the TESTA pathology in metacognitive form → the family is dead. |
| HELD | 0 < ΔM_trig < +8pp, or LCI straddles 0.05, or ΔM_trig > ΔM_rand numerically with p ≥ 0.05 | Positive but below the candidate's magnitude bar — licenses nothing, kills nothing. |
| PIVOT | I > 0 (I-screen passes) but ΔM_trig ≤ 0 | The signal is real but useless for revision — re-scope to diagnostic use only (never a revision trigger). (The backlog's bar, binding.) |

Precedence: KILL (any) > PIVOT > CONTINUE > HELD. The +8pp bar matches the program's standard capability bar (S4-4/S4-5 precedent).

## 4. Mathematical license

### 4.1 Where a self-modeling signal could come from [CONJECTURE as mechanism]

**[INTERPRETATION]** A frozen next-token model has no dedicated metacognitive module. But next-token training on human text exposes the model to the *statistical signature* of epistemic states: humans hedge, qualify, and correct themselves in text, and the contexts preceding correct vs incorrect claims differ systematically. Kadavath et al. (2022) provide the existence proof that this signature is partially readable: P(True) self-evaluation of own samples shows encouraging calibration at scale, and it is a *partially distinct skill* from answering (one-shot "is this true?" performs worse). The conjecture is that some of this signature survives at 410M parameters on this bench — not as reliable introspection, but as a weak statistical signal the I-screen can detect or kill.

### 4.2 Why â_self might survive TESTA's inversion — head-on

**[INTERPRETATION]** TESTA's mechanism: position bias makes the model lean first-listed; the margin (decision-local sharpness at the answer token) is calibrated to the biased *decision policy*, not to correctness. â_self differs in three load-bearing ways:
1. **Separate forward pass, separate question.** The margin is computed *in* the decision; â_self is computed *about* the decision, after it. The self-eval prompt fixes the proposed answer and asks a True/False question — the position bias that corrupted the original 2-option ranking now operates on a different choice (True/False, which we fix in a constant order), not on the answer options.
2. **Judgment, not sharpness.** The margin measures how peaked the decision distribution is; â_self measures the model's probability that a *completed* answer is correct given the item. A confidently-wrong decision (high margin, wrong answer) is precisely the case the self-eval is built to catch.
3. **Counterfactual leverage.** The revision stage uses the order-swap framing — the counterfactual that exposes the bias TESTA identified. If the self-model is even weakly informative, triggering revision where it disagrees with the decision concentrates the re-framing where the bias is most likely to have struck.

**[INTERPRETATION — the honest limit]** Kadavath's P(True) worked on models up to 52B parameters and was format-fragile; RLHF policies were miscalibrated. At Pythia-410m, the honest prior is that the signature is absent — the model may simply re-derive its biased answer when judging, in which case â_self is the bias wearing a second hat. That is exactly what G2/G3 test. The license is a [CONJECTURE]; the I-screen is the breaking instrument, and a null KILLs the family before any revision spend.

### 4.3 Self-correcting bars

**[INTERPRETATION]** The revision controls are the anti-Self-Refine confound kit: Self-Refine's reported gains partly came from prompt-design artifacts (Huang et al. show adding explicit criteria to the initial prompt beats the whole pipeline) and from resampling. Our revise-all and random-trigger controls isolate the *trigger's information* from reframing and resampling. If discrepancy-triggered revision cannot beat random revision, the trigger is decoration — and the ΔM bars self-correct (ΔM_trig ≤ ΔM_rand → KILL revision).

### 4.4 Quantitative prediction and breaking points

**Prediction (the candidate's):** I(â_self; c) > 0 with permutation p < 0.05; if the I-screen passes, ΔM_trig ≥ +8pp (paired LCI > 0.05) **and** ΔM_trig > ΔM_rand (paired one-sided p < 0.05).
**Breaking points (binding):** G2 null → KILL before revision; G3 either-inverted → KILL family; Stage-2 ΔM_trig ≤ ΔM_rand → KILL revision; ΔM_trig ≤ 0 despite I > 0 → PIVOT to diagnostic.

## 5. Calibration check design (Law #7 firewall)

**[FACT]** Bench labels are used **solely** to compute per-item correctness for instrument validation. **No self-eval score, no trigger, no revision, no gate threshold sees labels.** The firewall mirrors TESTA's: labels touch only the instrument, never selection.

**Instrument.** Per item: â_self_i (the signal; the trigger threshold τ_rev = 0.5 is fixed, never tuned on labels). Per-item correctness from bench labels.

**Pre-registered tests (Stage-1 CPU pilot, one-sided in the licensed direction — higher â_self ↔ higher accuracy):**
- **C1:** Spearman(â_self, per-item accuracy), one-sided p < 0.05, positive direction. (Mirrors TESTA's R3 with the sign in the licensed direction.)
- **C2:** TESTA-style partition — mean â_self on majority-wrong items < mean â_self on majority-right items, one-sided permutation test (100,000 permutations, seed pinned in pre-reg), p < 0.05.

**Gate logic (binding):** C1 and C2 both pass in the licensed direction → G3 passes → Stage 2 licensed (with G1, G2). Either null → G3 fails → no GPU (family stands down — signal absent). **Either significantly inverted (wrong-direction p < 0.05) → KILL the self-modeling family** — â_self carries the TESTA pathology in metacognitive form.

## 8. Prior art (searched 2026-10-03; abstracts/secondary verified — full-text verification is a pre-reg-stage follow-up)

1. **Kadavath et al. 2022 (arXiv:2207.05221) [VERIFIED via arXiv/secondary].** "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know" — P(True) self-evaluation of own samples; trained P(IK) value head; calibration format-dependent and scale-sensitive; RLHF policies miscalibrated. **Closest prior art for the I-screen.** Distinctions: (a) their models run to 52B — S4-6 tests whether any of the signature survives at 410M frozen; (b) their P(IK) head required training — S4-6 is training-free; (c) they did not test discrepancy-driven revision — the second stage is S4-6's novel element.
2. **Self-Refine — Madaan et al. 2023 (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2303.17651) [VERIFIED via arXiv].** Generate → self-feedback → refine loop with one frozen model; ~20% average improvement across 7 tasks. **Closest prior art for the revision stage.** Distinctions: (a) their feedback is free-form natural-language critique; S4-6's revision is *discrepancy-triggered* (revise only where the self-model disagrees) with matched resampling controls; (b) their tasks are generative — S4-6 tests decision rescue on a fixed bench with binding bars.
3. **Huang et al. 2024 (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.01798) [VERIFIED via arXiv/secondary].** "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet" — intrinsic self-correction fails and often degrades reasoning without external feedback; part of Self-Refine's gains traced to prompt-design artifacts. **The boundary this design must respect.** S4-6's answer: the discrepancy trigger is *not* free-form self-correction — it is a gated revision licensed only if the I-screen first establishes that the self-model carries information. If the I-screen is null, no revision runs — the design concedes Huang's point by construction.
4. **Kamoi et al. 2024 (TACL, REPORTED via secondary survey citation).** Meta-survey: prompt-only self-correction does not work in fair settings; works with reliable external feedback. Consistent with the design's gating structure; full-text verification flagged as follow-up.
5. **Xu et al. 2024 (ACL 2024, arXiv:2402.11436) [REPORTED via secondary].** "Pride and Prejudice" — self-bias in self-refinement (models rate their own output higher). Relevant confound for the self-eval: if â_self is inflated by self-bias, the trigger rarely fires — G1 (variance gate) and the trigger-rate diagnostic observe this; the bars self-correct.

**Novelty read:** likely **N1 (known combination)** — P(True)-style self-evaluation and self-refinement loops both have prior art. The differentiators are the discrepancy-triggered (rather than blanket) revision with matched resampling controls, the falsification-first I-screen that kills before revision, and the pre-registered calibration gate on this bench/backbone. The design claims none of the prior art's territory and cites it.

## 9. Risks and honest limitations

1. **Honest prior is against the signal** (§4.2): TESTA (anti-calibrated margin), EXP140 (null free energy), EXP125 (dead verbalized confidence), and Huang et al. (intrinsic self-correction fails at frontier scale) all point the same way. G2 exists because the prior says the self-model is probably absent at 410M.
2. **Self-bias confound** (Xu et al.): the model may rate its own answers highly across the board → â_self has no variance → G1 catches it, trigger never fires, I-screen is vacuous. The gates observe this; they do not patch it.
3. **The EXP125-distinction risk** (§6.v): if the reviewer judges the distributional-vs-verbalized distinction insufficient, this design should be REJECTED — stated plainly rather than laundered.
4. **Elicitation shift:** the self-eval prompt changes the decision context; â_self is comparable across items but absolute values are prompt-dependent. The bars use only relative informativeness (I, AUC, rank correlation), never absolute thresholds — except τ_rev = 0.5, which is a design parameter the pre-reg may revisit with justification.
5. **Bench specificity:** all bars are on the 60-item decision bench at Pythia-410m. No claim about other benches/backbones is licensed under any branch.
6. **â_self is sign-less until G2/G3** — EXP135's lesson is respected by making the sign channel a gate, not an assumption.
