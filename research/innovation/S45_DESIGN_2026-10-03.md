## 2. Decision changed

**What changes:** the architectural status of the free-energy-selector family — currently an untested J-tier conjecture and the program's only remaining candidate for a *training-free, target-free evaluator* (the verifier-bottleneck wound: every strong 2026 evaluator is trained). A surviving result licenses "model-evidence selects better than voting"; a failed one closes the family at <$0.01 T4-h.

**Free-energy estimator (pre-registered).** For branch k with rationale tokens r_k = (r_{k,1}, …, r_{k,|r_k|}) generated under branch prompt x_k:
- **F_k := −(1/|r_k|) · Σ_{i=1}^{|r_k|} log p_θ(r_{k,i} | x_k, r_{k,<i})** — the per-token mean negative log-likelihood (surprise density) of the rationale under the frozen model. **[DEFINITION]**
- Computed over **rationale tokens only** (primary): prompt tokens are excluded (branch prompts differ in option order — including them would contaminate the comparison), and the final answer token is excluded (keeps F a pure *trajectory* signal, outside the margin's decision-local territory). Full-trajectory F (including the answer token) is a pre-registered secondary diagnostic.
- The per-token normalization makes the estimator **λ-free** — no invented complexity weight (the S4 sketch's `λ|r_k|` is dropped; length is handled by the density, not a free parameter).
- Selection: **k* = argmin_k F_k**; ties broken toward the lowest branch index. The decision is branch k*'s forced-choice answer.

**Branch generators (pre-registered, K=4, prompt/decoding level).** The S4 sketch's "branch at l* with the S3-5 μ-probe set" is **stale** — S3-5 (ACG) was culled at LOG-4353, so that reference names a dead idea; this design re-specifies branching at the fully-observable prompt/decoding level (documented deviation, not improvisation):
- **B0** — anchor: greedy rationale (≤R_max tokens), then forced-choice answer.
- **B1** — framing counterfactual: option order swapped in the prompt (attacks the known position bias head-on), greedy rationale, forced-choice answer.
- **B2, B3** — stochastic counterfactuals: τ=0.7 sampling, two fixed seeds (design parameters), forced-choice answer.
- Rationale template (fixed, identical for all branches): `<item prompt> Reason step by step, then answer:` — the single-pass baseline B0 uses this same template, so ΔM isolates *selection*, not elicitation.
- Answer readout: argmax over {tok1(A), tok1(C)} at the answer position — the same 2-token readout as the EXP135 baseline (Law: no readout change across arms).

**Comparators (pre-registered).**
- **Random-branch:** uniform selection over the 4 branches, fixed seed `20261003` (design parameter). Expected ΔM_random ≈ mean-branch ΔM.
- **Self-consistency (forced, matched tokens):** majority vote over the 4 branch answers at the same K and token budget; ties broken toward B0's answer. (Forced baseline per SPRINT2_2026-09-23 §TIER-0: any capability claim beats SC at matched tokens.)

**Endpoints (pre-registered).** ΔM_FE = acc(min-F selection) − acc(B0); ΔM_random = acc(random-branch) − acc(B0); ΔM_SC = acc(majority vote) − acc(B0). Accuracies on the 60-item bench.

| CONTINUE | ΔM_FE ≥ +8pp with paired LCI > 0.05 **and** ΔM_FE > ΔM_random (paired one-sided p < 0.05) | Free-energy selection carries signal beyond branching and beyond voting. Licenses a follow-on (new number) on the selector mechanism. |

## 4. Mathematical license

### 4.1 Free energy as model evidence [CONJECTURE as mechanism]

**[DEFINITION — FACT as mathematics]** In the free-energy principle, variational free energy F = E_q[−log p(o,s)] − H[q] upper-bounds surprise −log p(o); minimizing F maximizes model evidence. Our F_k is the empirical per-token surprise density of a rationale trajectory under the frozen model — "how much the model's own distribution objects to each step of the reasoning."

**[INTERPRETATION]** p_θ encodes the model's learned regularities of coherent reasoning. A rationale that is internally inconsistent — premise contradicted by conclusion, evidence-straining, mid-trajectory hedging — visits tokens with low p_θ in context → high F. A rationale that *settles* (each step predictable from the last, given the item) → low F. Min-F selection = "select the trajectory the model's own world-model finds least surprising": model-evidence selection with no labels and no training.

### 4.2 Why free energy might survive TESTA's inversion — head-on

**[INTERPRETATION]** The margin m⁰ is a *decision-local* statistic (top-1 − top-2 logit gap at the answer token). TESTA's mechanism: position bias makes the model lean first-listed; when the correct answer is second, the bias points wrong *and the margin is larger* — the margin is calibrated to the model's *decision policy* (sharpness of the biased choice), not to correctness.

Free energy differs in three load-bearing ways:
1. **Trajectory-integrated, not decision-local.** F averages surprise over the whole rationale. The bias inflates *final-token* sharpness, but a rationale leading to a bias-driven wrong answer must rationalize against the item's evidence — those rationalization tokens are low-probability under the model's own distribution. The margin cannot see this; F integrates it.
2. **Density, not gap.** F measures the model's probability *mass* on the path, not the sharpness of one decision. A confidently-wrong final token can still sit at the end of a high-surprise trajectory.
3. **Counterfactual comparison.** B1 (order-swapped framing) exposes the framing-dependence of the bias: the selector compares the model's surprise *across framings* and can prefer the framing under which the model reasons with least strain — something a single-pass margin can never do.

**[INTERPRETATION — the honest limit]** F is still computed from the same frozen model's logits on the same bench family. If the model's *entire* generative distribution here tracks the biased policy (fluency = bias), F inherits the inversion — and min-F selection would be *worse* than random. That is exactly what G3 tests. The license is a [CONJECTURE]; G3 is the breaking instrument, and inversion KILLs the family.

### 4.3 Self-correcting bars

**[INTERPRETATION]** The temperature asymmetry (B2/B3 sampled at τ=0.7 have higher F *by construction*) means min-F may rarely select stochastic branches — the selector could collapse to "pick the least-strained deterministic branch." The design does not patch this: selection frequency per branch is a reported diagnostic, and the ΔM bars self-correct (if min-F always picks B0, ΔM_FE ≈ 0 → KILL branching). A selector that cannot beat its own anchor deserves to die.

### 4.4 Quantitative prediction and breaking points

**Prediction (the candidate's):** ΔM_FE ≥ +8pp (paired LCI > 0.05) **and** ΔM_FE > ΔM_random (paired one-sided p < 0.05).
**Breaking points (binding):** G3 null → no GPU; G3 inverted → KILL family; Stage-2 ΔM_FE ≤ ΔM_random → KILL selector; ΔM_FE ≤ 0 → KILL branching.

## 5. Calibration check design (Law #7 firewall)

**[FACT]** Bench labels are used **solely** to compute per-item correctness for instrument validation. **No branch score, no selection, no ranking, no gate threshold sees labels.** The firewall is identical to TESTA's: labels touch only the instrument, never selection.

**Instrument.** Per item: F̄ = mean_k F_k over the 4 branches (the *signal*; mean, not min — min is selection-adjacent). Per-item accuracy: majority-correct over the 4 branch answers (stable; labels only).

**Pre-registered tests (Stage-1 CPU pilot, one-sided in the licensed direction — lower F ↔ higher accuracy):**
- **C1:** Spearman(F̄, per-item accuracy), one-sided p < 0.05, negative direction. (Mirrors TESTA's R3, which found ρ=−0.3958 for the margin — the sign must go the *other* way here.)
- **C2:** TESTA-style partition — mean F̄ on majority-wrong items < mean F̄ on majority-right items, one-sided permutation test (100,000 permutations, seed pinned in pre-reg), p < 0.05.

**Gate logic (binding):** C1 and C2 both pass in the licensed direction → G3 passes → Stage 2 licensed. Either null → G3 fails → no GPU (family stands down, not killed — the signal may be absent rather than anti-informative). **Either significantly inverted (wrong-direction p < 0.05) → KILL the free-energy-selector family** — the signal carries the TESTA pathology and selection on it would be worse than random. Secondary diagnostic: same tests with min_k F_k.

## 8. Prior art (searched 2026-10-03; abstracts only — full-text verification is a pre-reg-stage follow-up)

1. **FREIA — Free Energy-Driven RL with Adaptive Advantage Shaping (arXiv:2605.04065) [REPORTED].** Free-energy principle as an unsupervised RL *reward* for LLM reasoning (consensus alignment + exploration). **Closest principle-level prior art.** Distinction: FREIA *trains* (policy optimization); S4-5 trains nothing (Δθ=0) — pure inference-time selection. Same principle, opposite sides of the training/inference line.
2. **Minimum Bayes Risk decoding (Jinnai et al., arXiv:2311.05263; Freitag et al., arXiv:2111.09388) [REPORTED].** Principled selection over sampled hypotheses — but MBR needs a utility/risk function, often a *trained* judge (Prometheus). S4-5's selector is judge-free (the model's own distribution). Note Freitag et al.'s warning — "model estimates and translation quality only vaguely correlate" — is the exact skepticism G3 operationalizes.
3. **MBR test-time scaling over trajectories (e.g., CycleVLA, arXiv:2601.02295) [REPORTED].** Sample K trajectories, select by expected risk under a distance metric. Distinction: needs a metric; free-energy selection needs only the model's surprise density.
4. **Self-consistency (forced comparator per SPRINT2).** Majority vote → S4-5's delta is vote → model-evidence selection.

**Novelty read:** likely **N1 (known combination)** — test-time trajectory selection has MBR prior art. The differentiator is the judge-free free-energy selector with falsification-first bars and a pre-registered calibration gate on this bench/backbone. The design claims none of the prior art's territory and cites it.

## 9. Risks and honest limitations

1. **Honest prior is against the signal** (§4.2): TESTA showed internal signals on this bench family track the biased policy — possibly *inverted*. G3 exists because the prior says the selector may be an anti-selector.
2. **Temperature vacuity** (§4.3): min-F may collapse to the deterministic anchor; the bars self-correct, but the most likely Stage-2 outcome may be KILL-branching, not CONTINUE.
3. **Elicitation shift:** the rationale template changes the decision context vs the direct-decode baselines; ΔM is clean (B0 shares the template) but absolute accuracies are not comparable to EXP135's 43.67%.
4. **Bench specificity:** all bars are on the 60-item decision bench at Pythia-410m. No claim about other benches/backbones is licensed under any branch.
5. **The S4 sketch deviation** (l*-branching → prompt-level branching) is documented in §2/§6.i; mid-layer branching is a separate design, not a fallback.
6. **F is still sign-less until G3** — EXP135's lesson is respected by making the sign channel a gate, not an assumption.
